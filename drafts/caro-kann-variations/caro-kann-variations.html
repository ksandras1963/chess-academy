Caro-Kann Variations

1.e4 c6 prepares ...d5 without shutting in the c8 bishop. After 2.d4 d5 White must decide what to do with the attacked e4 pawn: push it (the Advance), trade it (the Exchange and the Panov), or defend it (3.Nc3 or 3.Nd2, which lead to the Classical and Karpov lines, and 3.f3, the Fantasy). White can also skip 2.d4.

Main variations

White’s main ways to meet 1...c6 and 2...d5, and Black’s two main fourth moves in the Classical lines.

Classical Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Bf5

5.6%of Caro-Kann games reach 4...Bf5

B18 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Classical Variation. Also called the Capablanca Variation.

The game
The main line. Black gives up the center pawn so the c8 bishop can come out to f5 before ...e6 shuts it in.
White
Chase the bishop with Ng3, then gain kingside space with h4 and h5. Trade the bishop on d3, and castle queenside if you want to attack.
Black
Answer h4 with ...h6, trade the bishop on d3, then finish with ...e6, ...Ngf6 and ...Be7. Free your game with ...c5 or ...e5.
Watch out
After 5.Ng3 Bg6 6.h4, play 6...h6. The natural 6...e6? 7.h5 drives the bishop to f5, where the knight takes it and leaves Black with doubled f-pawns.

Karpov Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nd7

1.3%of Caro-Kann games reach 4...Nd7

B17 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Karpov Variation.

The game
Very solid. Black gets ready for ...Ngf6, so that if White trades knights, Black takes back with a knight and keeps a healthy pawn structure.
White
Develop with Nf3 and Bc4, or jump to g5 with 5.Ng5 to pressure f7 and e6. Castle and keep more space in the center.
Black
Play ...Ngf6, ...e6 and ...Bd6 or ...Be7, then castle. The c8 bishop can still come to f5 or g4. Free your game with ...c5 or ...e5.
Watch out
After 5.Qe2, don’t play 5...Ngf6?? because 6.Nd6 is smothered mate: the e7 pawn is pinned to the king. Play 5...Ndf6 instead.

Advance Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.e5

21%of Caro-Kann games reach 3.e5

B12 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Advance Variation. Lichess calls 3...c5 the Botvinnik-Carls Defense and 4.Nf3 the Short Variation.

The game
White gains space with e5 and takes f6 away from Black’s knight. Black attacks d4, the base of White’s pawn chain.
White
The calm Short system: Nf3, Be2 and castle, then Be3, c3 or c4. Sharper tries are 4.h4 (the Tal Variation) and 4.Nc3 with g4 (the Van der Wiel Attack).
Black
Hit d4 with ...c5, then pile up on it with ...Nc6, ...Qb6 and a knight on f5. Get the c8 bishop out before ...e6.
Watch out
After 3...Bf5 4.h4, the natural 4...e6?? loses the bishop to 5.g4 and h5.

Exchange Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.exd5 cxd5 4.Bd3

16%of Caro-Kann games reach 3...cxd5

B13 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Exchange Variation. Lichess calls 6.Bf4 the Rubinstein Variation.

The game
The pawns are traded on d5, and Black’s c-pawn recaptures toward the center. After 4.Bd3 White aims at Black’s kingside; the quieter 4.Nf3 is played just as often at club level. 4.c4, the Panov, has its own card.
White
With Bd3, c3 and Bf4 you get a Carlsbad structure: three queenside pawns against Black’s two. Aim at the kingside with Qb3 or Qc2, a knight on e5 and a rook lift.
Black
Develop with ...Nc6, ...Nf6, ...Bg4 and ...e6, then use the two queenside pawns to attack White’s three with ...b5 and ...b4, the minority attack.

Panov-Botvinnik Attack

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.exd5 cxd5 4.c4

3.9%of Caro-Kann games reach 4.c4

B13 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Panov Attack.

The game
White accepts an isolated d-pawn in return for fast, active development. Open, more tactical games.
White
Develop quickly and use the outposts on e5 and c5 to attack. Pushing c4–c5 instead gives White three queenside pawns against two.
Black
Develop fast, block the d-pawn with a knight on d5 and trade pieces. Endgames favor Black, because the isolated pawn becomes a weakness.

Two Knights Attack

1.e4 c6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Nf3

5.0%of Caro-Kann games reach 3.Nf3

B10 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Two Knights Attack. Lichess calls 3...Bg4 the Mindeno Variation.

The game
White develops both knights and delays d4, hoping for tricks. It often turns into a Classical-type position.
White
Keep the d-pawn flexible, ready for d4 or d3. Aim at f7 and the e-file with Qe2, Bc4, Ng5 and Ne5, and harass Black’s bishop with h3.
Black
Pin with ...Bg4. When h3 asks the question, take on f3, then play ...e6 and ...Nf6. Black is solid and comfortable.
Watch out
After 3...dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nf6 5.Qe2, don’t play 5...Nbd7?? because 6.Nd6 is smothered mate.

Fantasy Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.f3

2.1%of Caro-Kann games reach 3.f3

B12 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Maróczy Variation.

The game
White props up e4 with the f-pawn so that, if Black takes, fxe4 keeps two pawns in the center and opens the f-file.
White
Develop with Nc3, Be3 and Qd2, castle quickly, and use the f-file. Remember that f3 weakens the diagonals toward your king.
Black
Either play 3...e6 with ...Bb4, or take with 3...dxe4 and strike back with ...e5. Use the weak dark diagonals: ...Qh4+ and ...Bc5.
Watch out
After 3...dxe4 4.fxe4 e5, White must not take on e5: 5.dxe5? Qh4+ wins the e4 pawn with check.

Black’s other fourth move: 4...Nf6

After 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nf6 White usually trades knights with 5.Nxf6+, and Black chooses which pawn takes back.

Tartakower Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nf6 5.Nxf6+ exf6

2.6%of Caro-Kann games reach 5...exf6

B15 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Tartakower Variation.

The game
Black takes back with the e-pawn for quick, easy development and a safe king, at the cost of a structure that is worse in the endgame.
White
Develop with Bd3, Ne2 or Nf3, Qc2 and castle. Trade toward an endgame, where your healthy queenside majority matters and Black’s doubled f-pawns do not help.
Black
Develop at once with ...Bd6, ...O-O, ...Re8 and ...Qc7, and play for the initiative in the middlegame.

Bronstein-Larsen Variation

1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 Nf6 5.Nxf6+ gxf6

0.2%of Caro-Kann games reach 5...gxf6

B16 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Bronstein-Larsen Variation.

The game
Unbalanced. Black takes back toward the edge, accepting doubled f-pawns for the half-open g-file and active pieces.
White
Develop calmly, keep your king safe, and remember that your pawn structure is healthier: a 4-against-3 majority on the queenside counts in an endgame.
Black
Use the g-file for a rook and play actively with ...Bg4 or ...Bf5, ...e6, ...Qc7 and ...Nd7. Queenside castling is common.

White’s other second moves

White can skip 2.d4. 2.Nf3 is very common at club level, but it usually transposes to a main line.

2.Nf3

1.e4 c6 2.Nf3

24%of Caro-Kann games reach 2.Nf3

B10 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense.

The game
White develops first and keeps the choice of d4 for later. Most games transpose to a main line.
White
3.Nc3 reaches the Two Knights. 3.e5 is like the Advance, and 3.exd5 cxd5 4.d4 is an Exchange.
Black
Answer 2...d5. Against 3.e5, play ...Bg4 before ...e6. Against 3.exd5 cxd5 4.d4, develop with ...Nc6 and ...Bg4.

2.Bc4

1.e4 c6 2.Bc4

4.7%of Caro-Kann games reach 2.Bc4

B10 Lichess: Caro-Kann Defense: Hillbilly Attack.

The game
White puts the bishop on c4 before d4, where ...d5 hits it at once. Popular online for its traps.
White
Against 2...d5, 3.exd5 cxd5 4.Bb5+ trades off; 3.Bb3 dxe4 4.Qh5 is the Schaeffer Gambit.
Black
Answer 2...d5. After 3.Bb3 dxe4 4.Qh5, play 4...g6 and keep the extra pawn.

Black
Take the center with ...d5 and ...e5, develop with ...Bd6, ...Nf6 and ...O-O, and enjoy a free, comfortable game.

Sources and notes

How often: Lichess opening explorer, rated rapid and classical games between players averaging 1800 to 2199, retrieved 27 October 2026. Card figures are the share of the 13.6 million games after 1.e4 c6 that reach the named position by any move order. Because move orders transpose, some games count on more than one card: 2.Nf3 d5 3.Nc3 is also the Two Knights, 2.Nf3 d5 3.exd5 cxd5 4.d4 is also the Exchange with 4.Nf3, and 2.c4 d5 3.exd5 cxd5 4.d4 is also the Panov. The figures agree within 3% with the snapshot your app saved on 18 to 25 October 2026.

Names: ECO codes and names as Lichess gives them (lichess-org/chess-openings). Where Lichess uses a different name from the usual one, the card says so, for example the Fantasy, which Lichess calls the Maróczy Variation.

Lines: most lines follow your Opening Trainer’s Caro-Kann courses. The Tartakower, Bronstein-Larsen and Two Knights (3...Bg4) lines follow named Lichess lines and the moves club players choose most. Every line was checked for legal moves with chess.js, and each trap in a Watch out box was checked move by move. The plans are general guidance, not engine evaluations.
